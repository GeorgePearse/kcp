How much usable video gets through?

Real encoded bytes · TCP / KCP / UDP · isolated Linux netem links

Exploratory edge-fixture pilot, not a production streaming or QUIC benchmark. Each trial offers four seconds of 1080p video at 10fps. A one-second segment earns credit only after every byte arrives and its SHA-256 matches. Deadline: 1.5 seconds after segment release. Observation: seven seconds. Partial segments get no usable-video credit.

How to read this

Complete video includes late segments received within the observation window. On-time video measures segments that could meet the chosen playback deadline. Received payload can look healthy even when scattered losses make every segment incomplete. TCP and KCP recover losses but can build queues; UDP does not retry. More bytes received does not necessarily mean more usable video.

H.264: libx264 veryfast CRF23, no B frames. MJPEG: quality 4. Both 1920×1080 at 10fps, independently encoded one-second segments. Settings are not matched for perceptual quality. Encoding is performed before transport; capture and encoder latency are excluded. No decoder concealment is attempted. These are application-framed segment transfers, not RTP/RTSP.

Evidence and limits

Loss is unseeded. Two repeats are exploratory; shared-host CPU and kernel buffers affect results. Link limits apply per direction. Traffic counters include ACKs, transport headers and retransmissions but are not physical-wire measurements. There is no encryption, cross-traffic, GPU decoding or production GCS integration. The raw run retains input/segment hashes, decoder frame checks, arrival records, network commands and counters.
